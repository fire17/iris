Objective: make the trust layer visible. The product is the verification layer, not the content — so the badge system had to stop being an emoji prefix on a label and become the loudest, most deliberate thing in the shell.

What shipped

1 · The seal system

Verified Synthetic Demo

The signature move is the notch — every seal has one punched corner, the way a validated ticket or a customs stamp does, drawn with a two-layer clip-path so the 1px edge survives around the cut. Not a pill, not glass, not a side stripe.

  • Opaque by law. A seal has to stay legible on top of arbitrary poster art, so every plate is fully opaque — never translucent, never blurred.
  • Demo is deliberately lesser. Same silhouette, but hollow, grey, and a dashed glyph. A test source must never cosplay as a verified one. That asymmetry is the honesty law expressed in CSS.
  • Glyphs are inline SVG strokes in currentColor. Zero assets, zero webfonts, no CDN.

2 · The honest ledger dock

The legend and the registry status are one surface, not two widgets — what the badges mean and what is actually being watched belong in the same breath. Always visible bottom-left, collapsed to a title plus a live seal per populated lane; one click expands the full definitions.

3 · Skipped rows, surfaced as a warning

Malformed rows are counted and shown, styled amber rather than red: nothing is broken, some rows just could not be read, and hiding that would be the dishonest thing. The copy also says how to fix one.

The one open item — and why I stopped instead of guessing

Exact per-tile DOM seals are not achievable from the public wall API. WallView.getState() returns tiles:[{i,sx,sy,k,dw,dh,vis,src}], where sx/sy are the tile centre in CSS pixels. But the on-screen half-width is dw * scale * k * 0.5 (js/wallview.js:848) and scale is not exposed. The layouts set it anywhere from 0.34 to 1.15 (js/wallview.js:118,147), so a badge positioned from dw*k alone would sit visibly off its tile, worse the further down a row it is.

Shipping badges that drift off their cards would have been worse than shipping none, and it is exactly the kind of wrong guess that becomes load-bearing. So I built the seal system where the geometry is exact and handed the canvas half over.

Handoff to hp-motion (canvas owner)

Either of these unblocks per-tile seals in an afternoon:

  • Cheapest: add scale: t.scale to the object returned inside getState().tiles. One property. A DOM overlay can then compute exact rects and I can position seals from outside the canvas.
  • Better: expose getTileRects() returning {i, x, y, w, h} in CSS pixels for visible tiles only, so consumers never have to re-derive the projection.
  • Or draw them natively in the canvas using the lane palette above — the plate/ink/edge triples and the three glyph paths are all in js/registry.js’s LANES table, ready to read.

Note for whoever takes it: do not subscribe to WallView.onSelect or onHover from a second module — those are single-slot callbacks (cbSelect = cb), and js/app.js:360-361 already owns both. Registering again silently clobbers the app’s handlers. onLayout appears unclaimed.
